# Restaurant-Operation-Analysis
Analyzed restaurant operations using SQL to evaluate menu performance, pricing, order trends, and customer preferences. Identified the most and least popular dishes, examined high-value orders, and developed data-driven recommendations to improve menu decisions, optimize resources, and enhance customer satisfaction.


**Restaurant Operation Analysis Using SQL**
**Project Overview**

This project analyzes restaurant operations using SQL to evaluate menu performance, customer ordering patterns, and spending behavior. The analysis combines menu item and order details data to identify popular and unpopular dishes and generate actionable insights to support data-driven business decisions.
Project Objectives
The project is divided into three main objectives:

**Objective 1: Menu Items Analysis**
•	Examine the menu items dataset.
•	Determine the total number of menu items.
•	Identify the least and most expensive menu items.
•	Analyze Italian dishes and their prices.
•	Determine the number of dishes in each category.
•	Calculate the average price of dishes by category.

**Objective 2: Order Details Analysis**
•	Explore the order details dataset.
•	Determine the period covered by the data.
•	Calculate the total number of orders and items ordered.
•	Identify orders containing the highest number of items.
•	Determine how many orders contained more than 12 items.

**Objective 3: Customer Preferences and Ordering Patterns**
•	Combine the menu items and order details tables.
•	Identify the most and least ordered dishes and their categories.
•	Identify the highest-spending orders.
•	Examine the details of the highest-spending order.
•	Analyze the top five highest-spending orders to uncover customer purchasing patterns.
Tools and Technologies
•	SQL
•	MySQL
•	MySQL Workbench

**Key Findings**
•	Hamburger was the most frequently ordered item, with 622 orders.
•	Edamame ranked second, with 620 orders.
•	Chicken Tacos was the least ordered item, with 123 orders.
•	Potstickers recorded 205 orders.
•	The analysis examined menu pricing, category distribution, order volumes, and high-spending customer orders.

**Recommendations**
•	Maintain the availability and visibility of popular items such as Hamburger and Edamame.
•	Investigate the reasons for the low demand for Chicken Tacos and Potstickers.
•	Consider reviewing pricing, recipes, and promotional strategies for less popular dishes.
•	Use order trends to support inventory planning, reduce potential food waste, and improve resource allocation.
•	Continue monitoring menu performance to support informed business decisions.

**Conclusion**
This project demonstrates how SQL can be used to transform restaurant data into meaningful business insights. By analyzing menu performance, order patterns, and customer spending, restaurant management can make informed decisions to improve operational efficiency and customer satisfaction.
